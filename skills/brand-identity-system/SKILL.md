# Brand Identity System

Build a brand system that can drive decisions, not a moodboard that only sounds nice.

## Core insight

A useful brand system is a set of constraints. If it cannot tell you what copy, colors, layout, imagery, and behavior to reject, it is not a system yet.

## Operating loop

1. Define the market tension: what customers currently believe, what competitors repeat, and what the product wants people to believe instead.
2. Choose the brand position as a sharp tradeoff: premium vs accessible, expert vs friendly, playful vs serious, rebellious vs trusted.
3. Translate the position into five decision rules: voice, visual hierarchy, color, typography, imagery, and motion.
4. Write examples for high-leverage surfaces: homepage hero, product tagline, onboarding message, error message, social bio, and sales one-liner.
5. Add negative examples so future agents know what not to produce.
6. End with a one-page brand card that can be pasted into `README`, `DESIGN.md`, pitch docs, or future prompts.

## Decision rules

- If the brand claims speed, visual design should reduce cognitive steps: short copy, obvious CTA, low ornament.
- If the brand claims trust, proof and restraint matter more than novelty.
- If the brand claims creativity, show range through composition and language, not random gradients.
- If the brand serves experts, avoid over-explaining; if it serves beginners, explain without condescension.

## Quality bar

- The brand has an enemy or contrast, not just adjectives.
- Every visual choice maps back to positioning.
- Examples are specific enough to reuse in real product surfaces.

## Anti-patterns

- Listing traits like modern, clean, bold without behavioral rules.
- Copying a category leader and calling it inspiration.
- Creating a beautiful system that does not help anyone make decisions.
