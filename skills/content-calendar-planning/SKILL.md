# Content Calendar Planning

Plan content as an operating system, not a list of post ideas.

## Core insight

The best calendar starts from repeatable content pillars and production capacity. A calendar that ignores creation effort becomes fiction after week one.

## Operating loop

1. Define the audience, business goal, time horizon, channels, and team capacity.
2. Choose 3-5 content pillars mapped to pains, aspirations, objections, proof, and product education.
3. Set a cadence by asset cost: long-form, short-form, email, video, community, and repurposed assets.
4. Plan flagship pieces first, then derive smaller posts from them.
5. Assign each item an angle, format, CTA, owner, due date, status, and success metric.
6. Add a review workflow for accuracy, brand, legal/compliance, and publishing.
7. Reserve slots for timely content and customer proof.

## Repurposing rule

One strong idea should become many native assets, not many duplicated captions. Translate the idea for each channel's behavior.

## Output format

```markdown
## Pillars
- Pillar -> audience pain -> proof source -> CTA

## Calendar
| Date | Channel | Asset | Angle | Source asset | Owner | CTA | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
```

## Quality bar

- Calendar matches real production capacity.
- Each item has a purpose and CTA.
- Repurposing paths are explicit.

## Anti-patterns

- Scheduling daily output without a creation engine.
- Treating channels as copy-paste destinations.
- Filling dates with topics that do not support a goal.
