# Sales Call Prep

Prepare for the conversation you need, not the pitch you want to give.

## Core insight

A strong sales brief is built around hypotheses to test. The goal of the call is not to present everything; it is to confirm pain, buying process, urgency, and next step.

## Composio CLI rule

Use `composio-cli` when pulling CRM, Gmail, Calendar, Slack, docs, or notes context. Follow the loop: discover, inspect schema, link, dry-run writes, and do not update CRM or send follow-ups without approval.

## Operating loop

1. Identify account, attendees, roles, stage, source, and meeting goal.
2. Research company context, trigger events, likely initiatives, tech stack, and competitor signals.
3. Summarize prior interactions from authorized tools.
4. Write pain hypotheses and the evidence behind each.
5. Draft discovery questions that confirm pain, owner, impact, timeline, alternatives, and buying process.
6. Prepare proof points and objection responses tied to the account.
7. Draft next-step options and post-call follow-up template.

## Question ladder

- Situation: what is happening now?
- Pain: why is it costly?
- Impact: who cares and how much?
- Process: how would change happen?
- Decision: what would make this worth doing now?

## Output format

```markdown
## Account brief

## Pain hypotheses
| Hypothesis | Evidence | Question to test |
|---|---|---|

## Call plan
- Goal:
- Agenda:
- Proof:
- Objections:
- Next step:
```

## Quality bar

- Brief is account-specific.
- Questions test assumptions rather than leading the witness.
- Follow-up path is ready before the call.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing a generic company profile.
- Overloading the seller with trivia.
- Treating the demo as the default next step.
