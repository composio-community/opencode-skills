# Vendor Comparison Analysis

Choose vendors through explicit tradeoffs, not feature-list theater.

## Core insight

The best vendor is the one that fits the job, constraints, adoption path, and risk tolerance. Feature parity matters less than the cost of getting value.

## Operating loop

1. Define the decision: job to be done, must-haves, disqualifiers, budget, deadline, stakeholders, and risk constraints.
2. Assign weights before researching options.
3. Research official docs, pricing, reviews, integration support, security/compliance, implementation effort, and switching costs.
4. Score vendors against weighted criteria.
5. Identify hidden costs: migration, training, admin, limits, support tier, data export, lock-in, and procurement friction.
6. Recommend best fit, runner-up, and no-go options.
7. Prepare vendor questions for the final call.

## Decision lenses

- Time to first value.
- Fit for the most common workflow.
- Fit for the riskiest edge case.
- Integration and data portability.
- Trust, compliance, support, and lock-in.

## Output format

```markdown
## Decision criteria
| Criterion | Weight | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|

## Vendor comparison
| Vendor | Score | Strength | Risk | Best for | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Recommendation
```

## Quality bar

- Criteria are defined before conclusion.
- Recommendation includes tradeoffs.
- Unknowns become vendor questions.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing by brand recognition alone.
- Treating all features as equally important.
- Ignoring implementation and switching cost.
